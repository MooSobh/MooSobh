What EM adds to gravity and magnetics, and the natural hydrogen (H2) system

interpretation guide

Gravity maps density and magnetics maps magnetite. Neither sees water, porosity, clay or alteration. EM measures electrical resistivity, which is controlled by exactly those: pore water and its salinity, clay and alteration minerals, and connected magnetite, sulphides or graphite. EM adds the wet part of the picture.

Wadi Ghadir lies in a Neoproterozoic ophiolite belt (serpentinised peridotite, gabbro, metavolcanics); check the units against the published geological map. Natural H2 forms when water oxidises the ferrous iron of olivine and pyroxene: olivine + water → serpentine + magnetite + H2. A working H2 system needs a source rock and water, pathways, and a trap with a seal, or a surface seep. Gravity and magnetics outline the source rocks; EM tests where water is and where it moves. EM does not detect H2 gas itself: it detects the conditions that make H2 form and migrate.

Role of each target in the H2 system

Soil gas at every EM station

Measure H2 with CH4, CO2, He and Rn in 1 m probes at each TEM station; repeat at different times of day, since H2 flux varies daily. EM explains where the gas comes from.

Water samples

Sample springs and wells for dissolved H2, pH and Eh. Hyperalkaline water (pH 10–12) is the fingerprint of active serpentinisation.

IP on the shallow magnetic lines

Add time-domain IP on L1 and L2 to separate magnetite (H2 by-product) from sulphides and graphite.

Joint interpretation

Invert TEM and AMT with the gravity and magnetic geometry (cross-gradient coupling, as in notebook 08) so all four properties share their boundaries.

Synthetic test of this line

1-D SimPEG forward modelling and inversion at every station

Scenario: the gravity and magnetic inversions give the geometry; serpentinite (light and magnetic) is 30 Ωm when wet and actively altering, 800 Ωm when dry, in a 2000 Ωm host under 20 m of 300 Ωm cover. Each sounding is forward-modelled with noise and inverted twice (references 100 and 10 000 Ωm); the dashed line is the depth of investigation, where the two inversions part. Detectability is the largest response difference with and without the serpentinite, in noise units: above 3 it can be seen.

How deep each set-up sees

rules of thumb

TEM: Spies (1989), 0.55 (M ρ / η)1/5, capped by the diffusion depth at the 10 ms gate. AMT: Bostick depth 356 √(ρ / f). The vertical line is the host resistivity chosen above.

Expected resistivities

typical ranges

Basement host rocks are resistive, so good EM targets are conductors (sulphides, graphite, altered shear zones) at the contacts of the gravity and magnetic bodies.

Stations

1 · Reference line R1

Measure first over the quiet area: it gives the background resistivity and the noise level that set how deep the other lines can see.

2 · TEM lines, priority 1

Central-loop soundings, square loop of the listed side, ≥ 10 A, gates to ≥ 10 ms; stack until the late gates are stable. Lines may shift up to ~300 m to follow tracks; keep them across strike.

3 · Deep lines and AMT

400 m loops plus tensor AMT (10 Hz–10 kHz, 50 m dipoles, remote reference) on the deep targets, then the regional AMT traverse P1 along A–B.

Depth caveat

Target depths come from a potential-field inversion and are uncertain by a few hundred metres; compact and smooth models fit the data equally well. Saline wadi fill near the coast shortens TEM penetration.
