Each compact run starts with smooth L2 iterations, cooling β until the data misfit φd reaches its target (the number of data). From the dashed line on, IRLS re-weights the regularisation towards sparse norms while keeping the misfit near the target.

Data

Gravity: residual complete Bouguer anomaly (2.67 g/cm³, regional of 3 km upward continuation removed), 984 stations reduced to block medians of 400 m. Magnetics: total-field anomaly minus IGRF-14 and a plane, block medians. Data sit at the sensor heights above the Copernicus GLO-30 surface.

Mesh

cells; 400 m × 400 m × 100 m in the core, padding growing by 1.3 to the sides and below; cells above the topography are air. The explorer shows the core and the top 3.3 km.

Compact inversion

Sparse norms p = 0 on the model and p = 1 on its three gradients, solved by iteratively re-weighted least squares (SimPEG Sparse + UpdateIRLS) after a smooth L2 start. Bounds: Δρ ±0.3 g/cm³, χ 0–0.3 SI. Sensitivity weighting counters the decay of the kernels with depth.

Joint inversion

Both data sets in one objective with a cross-gradient term (λ = 10⁹) that favours parallel gradients of Δρ and χ, so boundaries coincide without tying values together. Units: thresholds ±0.02 g/cm³ and 0.005 SI.

Reading the models

Potential-field data do not determine a unique model. Compact and smooth models fit the data equally well and bracket the shapes the data allow. Magnetic models assume induced magnetisation along today's field. Cells farther than 2.5 km from any data point are unconstrained and hidden by default.
